## 1. Binding force by court and bench

**Supreme Court.** Article 141 — the law declared by the Supreme Court is binding on all courts within India. The Supreme Court is not bound by its own prior decisions, but a smaller bench cannot overrule a larger one; the course open to a bench that disagrees with a coordinate bench is a reference to a larger bench, not departure. Bench strength therefore belongs in the snapshot and again in section 9: a Constitution Bench holding and a two-judge holding are not equal currency, and where they conflict the larger bench prevails regardless of date.

Obiter of the Supreme Court is not strictly binding, but it carries high persuasive weight and High Courts ordinarily follow it. Say this rather than treating it as either binding or discardable.

**High Courts.** A High Court's decisions bind all courts and tribunals within its territorial jurisdiction. Decisions of one High Court are persuasive only, not binding, in another — a common and productive distinction in section 10 where the opponent's authority is from a different High Court.

Within a High Court: a Division Bench binds a Single Judge; a Full Bench binds a Division Bench. A coordinate bench that disagrees should refer the matter, not decide contrary. If a judgment appears to have done so, flag it — the conflict itself is an argument.

**Vertical position for the user's forum.** Section 9 should answer, concretely: is this binding on the court the user is appearing before, or only persuasive? Where the user has not said which forum, note both possibilities rather than assuming.

## 2. What is not precedent

- **SLP dismissed in limine, without reasons.** Not a declaration of law under Article 141, and the doctrine of merger does not apply — the judgment below stands on its own authority. See *Kunhayammed v. State of Kerala*, (2000) 6 SCC 359. If a dismissal carries reasons, those reasons may attract Article 141 to their extent; merger operates where leave is granted and the appeal decided.
- **Orders under Article 142.** Relief moulded to do complete justice in a particular case is routinely stated not to be a precedent. Flag any such order as non-citable for the proposition, however attractive the facts.
- **Consent orders and compromise decrees.** No adjudication, no ratio.
- **Interim orders**, stay orders, and orders expressly passed "in the facts and circumstances of this case" or "without being treated as a precedent" — take that reservation at face value and reproduce it in section 9.
- **Headnotes.** Publisher headnotes (SCC, AIR) are editorial, not part of the judgment, and cannot be cited as the ratio.

## 3. Per incuriam and sub silentio

A decision is *per incuriam* where it was rendered in ignorance of a binding statutory provision or a binding precedent that would have altered the result. It is not enough that the reasoning is weak or that a contrary view is available — the omission must be of something binding.

*Sub silentio* covers a point that passed without argument or consideration; a decision is not authority for a proposition that was neither raised nor decided.

Both belong in section 10 when supportable on the face of the text, and both must be argued with the specific omitted provision or authority named. Do not assert either speculatively: if the judgment does not disclose what was cited before the court, say that the material for a per incuriam argument is not visible in the text.

## 4. Identifying the ratio the Indian way

Indian courts follow the approach that a decision is an authority for what it actually decides, not for everything that appears to follow logically from it, and that a judgment must be read in the context of the questions that arose for decision — a line from a judgment cannot be read as a statute. *State of Orissa v. Sudhansu Sekhar Misra*, AIR 1968 SC 647, is the standard citation.

Practical consequence for section 6: tie the rule to the material facts and to the question actually framed. If the court was construing a specific sub-section on specific facts, the ratio is about that sub-section on those facts.

## 5. Tribunals

- NCLAT binds NCLT; CESTAT and ITAT benches are bound by the jurisdictional High Court, and a Special Bench decision carries greater weight than a Division Bench.
- For ITAT and similar, "jurisdictional High Court" matters more than the seniority of any other High Court — worth stating expressly in section 9.
- Tribunal decisions do not bind High Courts.
- Statutory appeals from tribunals often lie to the High Court or Supreme Court on questions of law only; where the case turned on a factual finding, that limits its use as authority. Note it.

## 6. Citation hygiene

- Give the citation exactly as it appears in the text. Do not reconstruct SCC or AIR references from memory — a wrong citation in a note becomes a wrong citation in a filing.
- Note the neutral citation (e.g. `2023 INSC 456`, `2022:DHC:1234`) if present; several courts now require it.
- Reportable / non-reportable is an internal marking. An unreported judgment is still a precedent; it simply needs a certified or downloaded copy for the court.
- **Subsequent history cannot be verified from the judgment text.** Whether the case has been overruled, doubted, referred to a larger bench, or stayed is invisible in the four corners of the document. Always close section 9 with a note that a citator check is required before the case is cited.

## 7. Statutory drift

Check whether the provision construed still reads the same way. Indian commercial and procedural statutes have moved substantially — the IBC and its thresholds, the Arbitration and Conciliation Act's successive amendments, the GST regime replacing service tax and excise, the criminal codes replacing the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act, and Companies Act 1956 provisions surviving in judgments applied to the 2013 Act.

If the judgment construes a provision that has since been amended or replaced, that belongs in section 9 as a citation caution and in section 10 as a distinguishing axis. Where you are unsure whether the provision has changed, say that the position should be confirmed against the current text rather than asserting either way.

## 8. Phrases to use in the note

Precise and standard, in preference to loose paraphrase:

- "binding on this Court under Article 141" / "persuasive only, being a decision of another High Court"
- "the SLP was dismissed in limine; the judgment below therefore stands unmerged"
- "arguably per incuriam of Section __, which does not appear to have been placed before the Court"
- "the observation at para __ was not necessary to the decision and is obiter"
- "the finding is one of fact and the appeal lay only on a question of law"
- "the provision has since been amended; the construction may not survive the amended text"
